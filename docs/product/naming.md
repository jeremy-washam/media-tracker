# Product naming history

- Status: **Imladris selected**
- Decision date: 2026-09-20
- Scope: product-facing name; technical compatibility identifiers are unchanged

## Decision

The product is named **Imladris**.

Imladris is Rivendell: a private refuge where stories, history, art, and
knowledge are gathered and preserved. That association fits the application as
a personal place for media history, exploration, reflection, and connections
across a large body of material. It also avoids sounding like a productivity
tool or commercial media service.

Use **Imladris** in the production interface, browser and API titles, CLI help,
and maintained product/design documentation. The repository path,
`@media-tracker/*` package scope, `media-tracker` storage and deployment resource
names, API service identifier, and existing local-storage keys remain stable
compatibility identifiers. Renaming them would be a separate infrastructure
migration, not part of the product-name decision.

## Naming brief

The exploration began with a private media-and-learning application that would
combine a trustworthy cross-media history with intentional discovery. The name
needed to feel personal, curious, and expansive rather than administrative or
SaaS-like.

The strongest metaphor supplied by the owner was **navigating a maze of content:
something unfathomably vast and awe-inspiring**. That shifted the exploration
from catalog and knowledge-tool language toward refuges, paths, maps,
labyrinths, stars, and immense spaces.

## Initial exploration

The first round tested names related to curiosity, connections, collections,
and discovery:

- **Threadline** — interests forming a continuous line through time
- **Sideways** — learning by following adjacent ideas
- **Tangent** — curious and exploratory
- **Lumen** — light and understanding
- **Commonplace** — the commonplace-book tradition
- **Elsewhere** — discovery beyond the familiar
- **Index** — personal catalog and wayfinding
- **Relay** — ideas moving between sources, topics, and time
- **Intake** — everything taken in
- **Mosaic** — individual media forming a larger picture
- **Trellis** — a structure on which ideas grow
- **Fieldnotes** — personal and observational
- **Undercurrent** — persistent themes beneath consumption
- **Drift** — controlled discovery
- **Orbit** — media and topics circling a personal center
- **Weave** — connections across disparate media and interests
- **Keystone** — a central personal knowledge system
- **Pollen** — culture and knowledge carried between interests
- **Folio** — a personal collection of works
- **Aperture** — a focused opening into a broad world

The strongest suggestions from this round were **Tangent, Threadline,
Commonplace, Mosaic,** and **Orbit**. They were not selected by the owner.

## Tolkien exploration

The next round explored names from Tolkien's legendarium. **Ithildin** was the
initial recommendation because its hidden writing becomes visible under the
right light, suggesting latent connections revealed across media history.

Names considered:

- **Ithildin, Amon Sûl, Anor, Eärendil, Tengwar, Mellon,** and **The Seeing
  Stone**
- **Aiya, Estel, Elanor, Niphredil, Laurelin, Telperion, Varda, Elentári,
  Menel,** and **Gil-Estel**
- **Lórien, Imladris, Eregion, Osgiliath, Minas Tirith, Henneth Annûn,
  Mithlond,** and **Andúnië**
- **Nenya, Mirrormere, Mírë, Lanthir, Fëa, Olórin, Námo, Tirion,** and
  **Eldamar**

The strongest subset at the time was **Ithildin, Estel, Lórien, Osgiliath,
Mirrormere, Henneth,** and **Nenya**. Imladris remained in the larger set and was
selected later.

## Maze, path, and vastness exploration

After the owner identified the maze-and-vastness metaphor, the next round
focused on finding a path through an infinite library or navigable cosmos:

- **Asterion** — strange, beautiful, and labyrinthine
- **Ariadne** — the thread that leads through the maze
- **Thread** and **The Thread** — the navigation metaphor directly
- **Daedalus** — maker of the labyrinth
- **Liminal** — a threshold between possible paths
- **Waymark** and **Wayfinder** — orientation in a large landscape
- **Chart** and **Atlas** — mapping a broad world
- **Orrery** — a navigable model of the cosmos
- **Asterism** — a recognizable pattern among innumerable stars
- **Meridian** — a line of orientation
- **Parallax** — understanding through a changed perspective
- **Vast** — the scale of the space directly
- **Fathom** — turning the unfathomable into an action
- **Abyssal** and **Deepwell** — depth and inexhaustibility
- **The Archive of Paths** and **Manyways** — many possible routes through a
  collection
- **Elsewhere, Farfield,** and **Noctiluca** — discovery and a guide in darkness
- **Labyrinth, The Labyrinth, Mappa, Index of Wonder,** and **Infinite Atlas**

The strongest suggestions from this round were **Asterion, Ariadne, Fathom,
Orrery, Asterism, Manyways,** and **Farfield**. **Asterion** emphasized a private,
strange, beautiful system; **Ariadne** emphasized help finding a way through it.

The owner explicitly liked **Atlas** and **Vast**. A point-in-time usage check
found both names crowded, particularly Atlas. Vast was considered the better
private working name. Variants explored were **Vasta, Vastwell, Vastward,
Vastfold, Vastline, The Vast, Vastfield, Atlas of the Vast, Far Atlas,** and
**Deep Atlas**. **Vastward** was the strongest variant because it suggested
movement outward into a vast space. That usage research is historical and must
be refreshed before relying on it for any public release, domain, or trademark
decision.

## Later placeholder

The interface studies temporarily used **Margin** as mock branding. It was
always documented as a placeholder and was never an accepted naming decision.
The earlier **Media Tracker** label was likewise a descriptive repository and
working name rather than a product identity.

## Final selection

On 2026-09-20, the owner returned to the earlier Tolkien list and selected
**Imladris**. Its refuge-and-preservation association fits the product's current
Library, Chat, and Recaps direction better than the more feed-centric premises
behind some of the earliest candidates. Imladris now supersedes both Media
Tracker and Margin in product-facing use.
